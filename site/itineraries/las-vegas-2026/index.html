By Tony & Sharon · Traveled January 2026

This Las Vegas trip balanced the iconic with the unexpected: a Styx concert at the Venetian Theater, immersive art at Meow Wolf's Omega Mart, the jaw-dropping red rock landscape of Valley of Fire, steakhouse dinners, an ice bar, and the best free spectacles on the Strip. Four days, three nights, one rental car, and a lot of ground to cover.

We made The Venetian our home base. The Grand Canal Shoppes, Grand Lux Cafe and Rosina Cocktail Lounge are all steps from the room, and the Strip in both directions is walkable from the front entrance. The rental car stayed in the free self-park garage for our early desert mornings: Valley of Fire on Sunday, then Seven Magic Mountains and Jean Dry Lake Bed on Monday.

The plan, day by day

Day 1 Fri, Jan 30 · Las Vegas

Arrival day – welcome to Vegas

An evening flight, a rental car, and a first look at the Strip before an early night. Saturday is a full day.

  1. EveningFly from Newark to Harry Reid International (LAS)
  2. On arrivalTake the rental car shuttle from the terminal and pick up the car we'd use for all three desert outings Booked ahead
  3. Late eveningCheck in at The Venetian, then a short stroll through the Grand Canal Shoppes or along the Strip – even 20 minutes sets the tone for the weekend Booked ahead

Tip: The Venetian's attached self-park garage is free for hotel guests; we used it instead of valet to keep the car handy for early departures.

Day 2 Sat, Jan 31 · Las Vegas

Omega Mart, the Strip & Styx

A morning inside Meow Wolf's surreal supermarket, a free-spectacle walk down the Strip, burgers at the Flamingo, and Styx at the Venetian Theater.

  1. 8:30 AMBreakfast at Grand Lux Cafe, right inside the Venetian – the Cheesecake Factory's upscale sibling, with a big all-day menu
  2. 10:00 AMMeow Wolf Omega Mart at Area 15 (timed entry, a 10-minute drive) – an immersive art world hidden inside a surreal supermarket. Allow at least 90 minutes, follow the story and open every door Booked ahead
  3. 12:00 PMExplore the rest of Area 15 with Level 0 passes – more immersive installations, axe throwing, VR and more, if energy allows Booked ahead
  4. 1:00 PMSupply run for water and snacks (for the room and for Valley of Fire), then freshen up at the hotel
  5. 2:30 PMStrip walk: Palazzo shops → Wynn Conservatory (free seasonal floral display) → coffee at Urth Caffé → the Forum Shops at Caesars (Trevi Fountain replica and animated Roman statues) → the free Flamingo Wildlife Habitat
  6. 5:30 PMDinner at Gordon Ramsay Burger at the Flamingo – the Hell's Kitchen burger and truffle fries are the standouts
  7. 7:00 PMMore Strip exploring on the way back toward the Venetian, including the Bellagio Fountains (every 15 minutes after 8 PM)
  8. 8:30 PMStyx at the Venetian Theater Booked ahead
  9. 10:30 PMA round on the Venetian casino floor or a drink at Rosina Cocktail Lounge before calling it a night

Tip: The Flamingo Wildlife Habitat is free and genuinely surprising – Chilean flamingos, African penguins and koi behind a casino. Easy to spend 20 minutes there.

Day 3 Sun, Feb 1 · Valley of Fire

Valley of Fire & a night at The LINQ

An early start for the desert light at Valley of Fire State Park, a picnic among the sandstone, the Fire Wave hike, then back to the Strip for steak, magic and the High Roller.

  1. 8:30 AMQuick Starbucks and pack all the water and snacks – the park has limited facilities
  2. 9:00 AMDrive about an hour northeast on I-15, then Route 169 into the park (entry fee at the gate)
  3. 10:00 AMBeehive Rock (~15 min) – sandstone cross-bedding whose grooves shift direction as you look
  4. 10:20 AMAtlatl Rock (~20 min) – petroglyphs carved 2,000–4,000 years ago, seen up close from a metal staircase
  5. 10:45 AMPicnic lunch at Seven Sisters (~40 min) among towering sandstone formations
  6. 11:30 AMRainbow Vista (~15 min) – swirling, painted-looking rock and a short loop toward the Fire Canyon overlook
  7. 11:50 AMFire Canyon Overlook (~10 min) – red-and-white sandstone with Silica Dome in the distance
  8. 12:05 PMElephant Rock (~20 min) – a short walk to the best view of the park's most photogenic landmark
  9. 12:30 PMFire Wave (~60 min) – the highlight: a striped red, white and pink sandstone wave, about 1.5 miles round trip, easy to moderate
  10. 2:30 PMDrive back to the Venetian to shower and change
  11. 4:30 PMDinner at Smith & Wollensky's in the Grand Canal Shoppes – classic American steakhouse with an impressive wine list Booked ahead
  12. 7:00 PMMat Franco – Magic Reinvented Nightly at The LINQ, the America's Got Talent winner's intimate magic show
  13. 8:45 PMOptional: Minus 5 Ice Bar at The LINQ – everything kept at -5°C, with thermal coats and gloves provided (about 45 minutes); the speakeasy next door is the warm alternative
  14. 10:00 PMHigh Roller Observation Wheel – 550 feet tall, a 30-minute rotation over the Strip lit up at night
  15. 11:00 PMWind down at the Bellagio: the fountains and the free, 24/7 Conservatory, dressed for Lunar New Year

Tip: Bring at least 2 liters of water per person – the desert is dehydrating even in January. The Fire Wave photographs best when the sun is low, morning or late afternoon.

Day 4 Mon, Feb 2 · Las Vegas

Desert art, Fremont Street & a final dinner

Early photos at Seven Magic Mountains and Jean Dry Lake Bed, lunch in the Arts District, a steakhouse send-off at the Venetian, and a last night downtown.

  1. 8:00 AMQuick Starbucks and go early – fewer people and better light
  2. 8:30 AMSeven Magic Mountains, about 30 minutes south on I-15 – Ugo Rondinone's seven neon-painted boulder towers, up to 35 feet tall. Free and open 24 hours
  3. 9:30 AMJean Dry Lake Bed, a short drive away – a vast white playa for wide-open desert shots and long morning shadows
  4. 11:00 AMWelcome to Las Vegas sign, if there's room in the dedicated lot at the south end of the Strip
  5. 11:30 AMPinball Hall of Fame (free to enter, pay per game) or PlayPlayground at Luxor, a multi-story interactive venue
  6. 1:00 PMLunch at Esther's Kitchen in the Arts District – wood-fired, Italian-influenced cooking and excellent pasta Booked ahead
  7. 2:30 PMOptional Arts District stroll: Container Park and Ferguson's Motel
  8. 3:00 PMBack to the Venetian for the pool if it's heated and open – or last-minute Sphere tickets for The Wizard of Oz at 5 PM
  9. 7:00 PMFinal dinner at Delmonico Steakhouse, Emeril Lagasse's flagship inside the Venetian Booked ahead
  10. 8:30 PMUber downtown to the Fremont Street Experience: the free LED canopy shows on the hour, Don't Tell Mama piano bar, Vegas Vickie's Cocktail Lounge, the $1 million photo at Binion's, the Silver Strike coin machine and Pink Box Donuts at The Plaza, and the gold nugget at the Golden Nugget

Tip: Get to Seven Magic Mountains as early as you can – the low morning light makes the colors pop, and by 10 AM the tour groups often arrive.

Where we stayed

  • The Venetian

    Las Vegas Strip · 3 nights

    Grand Canal Shoppes, Grand Lux Cafe, Rosina and Delmonico are all steps from the room, the Strip is walkable in both directions, and self-parking is free for hotel guests.

Where we ate

The Venetian

  • Grand Lux Cafe Breakfast

    The Cheesecake Factory's upscale concept with an extensive all-day menu – a proper sit-down breakfast without leaving the hotel. Walk in.

  • Delmonico Steakhouse Dinner

    Emeril Lagasse's flagship steakhouse – aged beef, Creole-influenced sides and a beautifully designed room. A strong, no-Uber-needed final dinner.

  • Rosina Cocktail Lounge Drinks

    Classic cocktails in a relaxed but upscale room – not a club, not a dive. Exactly the right vibe after a long day; no reservation needed.

The Flamingo

  • Gordon Ramsay Burger Dinner

    Casual and satisfying, and exactly right before a two-hour concert. The Hell's Kitchen burger and truffle fries are the go-to order.

Grand Canal Shoppes, The Venetian

  • Smith & Wollensky's Dinner

    Classic American steakhouse with excellent cuts and an impressive wine list. The dinner menu starts at 5 PM, so book for 5 or later if you want it.

Arts District

  • Esther's Kitchen Lunch

    One of Las Vegas's most acclaimed non-casino restaurants: wood-fired, Italian-influenced cooking, excellent pasta and a lively room. Off-Strip, so drive or Uber; reservation recommended.

The Cosmopolitan

  • Milk Bar Dessert

    Famous for Cereal Milk soft serve. Open late, inexpensive and genuinely delicious after the Bellagio fountains or a Strip walk.

  • The Chandelier Lounge Drinks

    A stunning three-story lounge inside a massive chandelier installation. Craft cocktails and a grown-up energy – one of the best bars in Las Vegas.

Delano Las Vegas

  • Skyfall Lounge Drinks

    A 64th-floor lounge with some of the best panoramic Strip views from any bar. Calm and grown-up – ideal for a sunset cocktail.

Las Vegas Strip

  • Giordano's Pizza

    Chicago-style deep dish pizza – hearty and familiar for a casual dinner when nobody wants another steakhouse.

Planet Hollywood

  • Earl of Sandwich Quick eats

    Hot pressed sandwiches, fast service and low prices – a reliable grab-and-go between Strip activities.

The Plaza, Fremont Street

  • Pink Box Donuts Dessert

    Creative donut flavors and an inexpensive stop during a Fremont Street evening.

Book these ahead

  • The Venetian (or your Strip hotel of choice)
  • Rental car from Harry Reid airport – you'll need it for Valley of Fire, Seven Magic Mountains and Jean Dry Lake Bed
  • Concert tickets for the Venetian Theater
  • Meow Wolf Omega Mart at Area 15 (timed entry) and Area 15 Level 0 passes
  • Smith & Wollensky's, Esther's Kitchen and Delmonico Steakhouse (all on OpenTable)
  • Mat Franco – Magic Reinvented Nightly at The LINQ (sells out regularly)
  • High Roller Observation Wheel at The LINQ
  • Minus 5 Ice Bar time slot at The LINQ
  • PlayPlayground at Luxor (buy online in advance)
  • Sphere – last-minute tickets are sometimes available, so check the website or box office on the day
  • Lion Habitat Ranch and Red Door Escape Rooms, if you have extra time

Our tips

  • Rent a car for the desert

    The Strip is walkable and Uber covers the rest, but Valley of Fire, Seven Magic Mountains and Jean Dry Lake Bed all need a car. Pick it up via the rental car shuttle at Harry Reid and park free in the Venetian's self-park garage.

  • Layer up in January

    Days run 55–62°F, which is comfortable for exploring, but nights can drop to 38°F. Desert mornings are cold and afternoons warm, so bring a proper jacket. Check whether the pool is heated before planning a swim.

  • Prep for Valley of Fire

    It's an hour from the Strip with minimal facilities. Pack at least 2 liters of water per person plus snacks, bring sunscreen even in January, wear comfortable walking shoes for the Fire Wave, and have cash or card for the entry fee. Aim to leave by 9 AM for the best light.

  • Mind the steakhouse menu switch

    Smith & Wollensky's serves its lunch menu until 5 PM. If you're booked earlier, ask to be seated closer to 5 or check with the host about the dinner menu.

  • The best things on the Strip are free

    The Wynn Conservatory's seasonal display, the Forum Shops' Trevi Fountain, the Flamingo Wildlife Habitat, the Bellagio Fountains and Conservatory (open 24/7 – the Lunar New Year display is one of its best), and the Fremont Street canopy shows all cost nothing.

  • Go early for desert photos

    Seven Magic Mountains is best in soft, low morning light before the tour groups arrive around 10 AM. The Fire Wave looks richest in the morning or late afternoon; midday light flattens the rock.

  • If you have extra time

    Lion Habitat Ranch in Henderson is a nonprofit sanctuary for more than 40 African lions, and Red Door Escape Rooms at the Grand Bazaar Shops offers private rooms (Declassified, Enchantment or Survive). Book both in advance.
