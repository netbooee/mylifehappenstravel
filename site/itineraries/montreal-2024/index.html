By Tony & Sharon · Traveled August 2024

Montreal is close enough to drive, so we made it a family road trip. We left after work on Wednesday, slept in Saratoga County to break up the drive, and were in Old Montreal by lunchtime on Thursday. Le Saint-Sulpice Hotel, beside the Notre-Dame Basilica, put us within walking distance of almost everything we wanted to do.

Each day had one anchor. Thursday was the classic first-timer's loop: smoked meat at Schwartz's, a walk up Mount Royal for the views, the Museum of Fine Arts and an evening on Place Jacques-Cartier. Friday stayed at the Old Port with a boat ride, the Ferris wheel and a zipline for the boys, then a ghost tour after dinner. Saturday we took the ferry across to Parc Jean-Drapeau for a food festival before an evening in the Village.

We booked the dinners and the timed activities ahead and kept a rain plan and a few backup ideas in our pocket. On the way out of town on Sunday we settled the great Montreal bagel argument for ourselves.

The plan, day by day

Day 1 Wed, Aug 21 · Malta, New York

Head north and break up the drive

A short evening drive to get a head start, so Thursday morning is only the last few hours to the border and Montreal.

  1. 6:30 PMLeave home and drive to the Hyatt Place Saratoga/Malta
  2. Check in at the Hyatt Place Saratoga/Malta and relax for the night Booked ahead

Tip: Before leaving the house, make sure everyone has their passport. You need one for each person to cross into Canada by car.

Day 2 Thu, Aug 22 · Montreal

Schwartz's, Mount Royal & an evening in Old Montreal

We arrive around lunchtime and spend the afternoon on foot: lunch on the Plateau, a climb up Mount Royal for the views over the city, the Museum of Fine Arts on the way back down, then dinner at the Old Port.

  1. 7:30 AMBreakfast at the hotel
  2. 8:30 AMLeave the hotel and drive to Montreal, crossing the border on the way
  3. 12:00 PMArrive at Le Saint-Sulpice Hotel, 414 Rue Saint-Sulpice, check in and head out for lunch Booked ahead
  4. If it's raining: lunch in the Underground City, then the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts
  5. If it's dry: walk to Schwartz's Deli, 3895 Boulevard Saint-Laurent, for Montreal smoked meat sandwiches for lunch. It's a long walk, so take an Uber if we're really hungry
  6. If the line at Schwartz's is too long, walk about a mile to La Banquise for poutine instead, then walk around La Fontaine Park next door
  7. After Schwartz's, walk up into Mount Royal Park: the Kondiaronk Belvedere, the big paved lookout in front of the Mount Royal Chalet, for the best photo over downtown, then on to Beaver Lake
  8. Walk down to the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts (Musée des beaux-arts de Montréal, open 10 AM to 5 PM)
  9. Walk back to Le Saint-Sulpice Hotel along Saint Catherine Street
  10. If we still have time and energy: Uber to the Biodôme or the Montreal Botanical Garden (both open 9 AM to 6 PM)
  11. 6:30 PMWalk to Place Jacques-Cartier, a lively square with street performers and cafés
  12. 7:45 PMDinner at Vieux-Port Steakhouse & Terrasse Booked ahead
  13. End the day with a slow walk along the Old Port promenade to see the city lights

Tip: Watch the forecast. The Underground City and the Museum of Fine Arts make an easy rainy-day swap for the walk up Mount Royal.

Day 3 Fri, Aug 23 · Montreal

Boat, Ferris wheel & zipline at the Old Port, then a ghost tour

A day at the water's edge. Everything at the Old Port is a short walk from the next thing, so we line up the boat ride, the Ferris wheel and the zipline back to back, then wander Bonsecours Market and Rue Saint-Paul before dinner and a ghost tour after dark.

  1. 9:00 AMWalk to breakfast at a café nearby: Olive et Gourmando or Le Petit Dep
  2. 10:40 AMArrive at the Old Port for our 11:00 AM boat ride on the river with Le Petit Navire Booked ahead
  3. 12:30 PMRide La Grande Roue de Montréal, the big Ferris wheel at the Old Port. We printed our tickets for four Booked ahead
  4. 1:00 PMTyrolienne MTL Zipline for the boys. The tickets aren't for a set time, so we go whenever it suits Booked ahead
  5. See the Clock Tower and Clock Tower Beach
  6. Lunch or snacks around Bonsecours Market
  7. Stroll Rue Saint-Paul from east to west, Old Montreal's oldest street
  8. Back to the hotel to get ready for dinner
  9. 6:15 PMDinner at Gaspar Brasserie Française Booked ahead
  10. Walk to the meeting point for the ghost tour
  11. 8:30 PMOld Montreal ghost tour through the old streets after dark Booked ahead

Tip: Get to Le Petit Navire about 20 minutes early. The Ferris wheel and the zipline are right next to each other, so the rest of the morning falls into place.

Day 4 Sat, Aug 24 · Montreal

Ferry to Parc Jean-Drapeau & a night in the Village

We cross the river by ferry to Parc Jean-Drapeau, the island park left over from Expo 67, to see the Biosphère and catch Les Rendez-vous gourmands, a one-day food festival for the park's 150th anniversary. Back in town, we walk the car-free stretch of Saint Catherine Street East before dinner.

  1. 9:45 AMLeave for breakfast at a café nearby
  2. 11:15 AMWalk to the ferry dock at the Old Port and be there 15 minutes early
  3. 11:30 AMFerry across to Parc Jean-Drapeau Booked ahead
  4. Walk around the park: Espace 67 and the Biosphère. The free Discovery Ride trams go across the island if we get tired of walking
  5. 12:00 PMLes Rendez-vous gourmands in the Floralies Gardens, 25 Chemin des Floralies: an outdoor food festival with chefs, tastings, barbecues and music, on from noon to 11 PM
  6. 3:45 PMBack at the ferry dock for the 4:00 PM ferry to the Old Port. Roads run close to the gardens, so an Uber back to the hotel works if we want to leave earlier or stay later Booked ahead
  7. Freshen up at the hotel if we're tired
  8. Walk to Saint Catherine Street East in the Village, closed to cars between Saint-Hubert and Papineau, with shops, food and street entertainment
  9. 7:45 PMDinner at Jacopo Booked ahead
  10. If we're still up for it: the bars on Crescent Street

Tip: If the Village is too much fun to leave, cancel the Jacopo reservation on OpenTable in time and eat there instead.

Day 5 Sun, Aug 25 · Montreal

Bagel wars on the way home

A slow morning, then the most important decision of the trip. Montreal has two famous bagel bakeries a few blocks apart in Mile End, and we stop at both on the drive out to see which one wins.

  1. 12:00 PMCheck out of Le Saint-Sulpice Hotel
  2. Bagel wars: drive to Mile End and compare the bagels from Fairmount Bagel and St-Viateur Bagel
  3. Drive home

Tip: Buy an extra dozen of the winner for the car. Montreal bagels are best warm, but they're still good the next morning.

Our tips

  • Passports for everyone

    Driving into Canada means a border crossing, and every person in the car needs a passport. Check them before you leave home, not at the hotel the night before.

  • Break up the drive

    Leaving after work and sleeping halfway meant we reached Montreal by lunchtime on Thursday with a full afternoon ahead of us, instead of losing the whole first day to the car.

  • Stay in Old Montreal

    From Le Saint-Sulpice we walked to the Old Port, Place Jacques-Cartier, Rue Saint-Paul and most of our dinners. We used Uber for the farther sights, like the Biodôme and the Botanical Garden.

  • Have a rain plan

    The Underground City links shops and food courts below downtown, and the Museum of Fine Arts is nearby. Both are good swaps for a wet afternoon.

  • Look for the summer pedestrian streets

    In summer, Montreal closes several streets to cars, including Saint Catherine Street East in the Village. Check the city's list before you go for more places to wander.

  • More ideas for a Saturday with kids

    The Geordie Family Fair was on that Saturday from 11 AM to 6 PM, free and about a 15-minute drive from Old Montreal. Check for family events on the dates you visit.
