By Tony & Sharon · Traveled July 2026

We gave Barcelona two packed days and saw as much of it as we could on foot: Montjuïc, La Rambla and the Gothic Quarter, El Born, Passeig de Gràcia and the port on the first day, then Sagrada Família, the Passion Tower, Sant Pau and Park Güell on the second.

Then we took an early flight to the island for five slow days at Zafiro Palace Andratx on Mallorca's southwest coast. We booked a single tour for the week and left every other day open, then flew back to Barcelona for one last night in the city before heading home.

The plan, day by day

Day 1 Thu, Jul 2 · Barcelona

Montjuïc, the Old City and the port

We landed, dropped our bags and walked straight out the door: a loop from the hotel on Montjuïc down through the medieval core, up to Passeig de Gràcia and back by cable car.

  1. 9:00 AMLand in Barcelona; customs and immigration, then a taxi or Uber to the hotel
  2. 10:30 AMCheck in at the InterContinental Barcelona (if the room isn't ready, they'll hold the bags). It has a rooftop pool and bar. Booked ahead
  3. 11:00 AM–12:15 PMMontjuïc on foot, 5–10 minutes from the hotel
  4. Palau Nacional: the palace built for the 1929 International Exhibition, now the National Art Museum of Catalonia. Even without the museum, the Oval Hall and the terrace views are worth it.
  5. Magic Fountain of Montjuïc: free and 1929-era. The water, music and light shows only run in the evening; by day it's mostly dormant.
  6. 12:15 PMUber or taxi to La Boqueria (10–15 minutes)
  7. 12:30–3:00 PMLa Rambla and the Gothic Quarter on foot, one of the prettiest walks in the city
  8. La Boqueria, the huge historic food market, then down La Rambla
  9. Plaça Reial: a 19th-century neoclassical square with palm trees, busy terraces and street lamps by a young Gaudí
  10. Gothic Quarter: medieval lanes, Roman ruins, tapas bars and artisan shops; a good place for lunch
  11. Plaça Sant Jaume, home of the town hall, and Barcelona Cathedral
  12. 3:00–4:30 PMEl Born to the Arc de Triomf on foot
  13. Mercat de Santa Caterina, the El Born neighbourhood and the Gothic basilica of Santa Maria del Mar
  14. Parc de la Ciutadella, on the site of a former military citadel
  15. Arc de Triomf: the red-brick gateway to the 1888 Universal Exposition, then a stroll down the palm-lined promenade
  16. 4:30–5:30 PMTaxi to Casa Batlló (about 25 minutes on foot otherwise), then walk Passeig de Gràcia: Casa Batlló and Casa Milà (La Pedrera) from the outside, plus 150+ designer boutiques in historic buildings
  17. 5:30–6:30 PMUber or taxi to Port Vell, the oldest part of the port, rebuilt for the 1992 Olympics: Maremagnum, IMAX Port Vell and Europe's largest aquarium
  18. 6:30–7:30 PMPort Cable Car up to Montjuïc for views and photos, then walk back to the hotel
  19. 7:30–8:15 PMBack to the hotel to relax
  20. 9:00 PMDinner at La Gastronómica Tapas, about a 15-minute walk from the hotel (reserved on TheFork) Booked ahead
  21. 10:30 PMWalk back and bed

Tip: If the cable car line is long, skip it and taxi back; if you're running early, add Montjuïc Castle (check it's still open).

Day 2 Fri, Jul 3 · Barcelona

Gaudí, Modernisme and the view from the top

Timed entries all morning, a hidden Art Nouveau campus at lunch, Park Güell in the afternoon and a late dinner at Casa Amàlia 1950.

  1. 8:45 AMTaxi to Sagrada Família, aiming to be at the entrance by 9:30
  2. 9:45 AM–12:15 PMSagrada Família tour. The 9–10 AM Quiet Hour means the basilica is silent when you walk in, and by 10:00 the morning sun lights up the blue, green and yellow windows of the eastern Nativity facade. We spent the first hour on the east side of the nave before the sun climbed. Booked ahead
  3. 11:15 AMClimb the Passion Tower: elevator up, then a very narrow, winding spiral staircase down Booked ahead
  4. 12:00 PMMuseum and gift shop under the Passion facade; from the basement museum a small window looks down on Gaudí's tomb in the crypt
  5. 12:15–1:15 PMQuick bite nearby: TuscaniaLab on the plaza, Buenas Migas or the Mercat de la Sagrada Família
  6. 1:15–2:15 PMRecinte Modernista de Sant Pau (1:30 PM tickets, a 10-minute walk), the world's largest Art Nouveau complex and a UNESCO site: a former hospital built 1905–1930 as a garden city of light, air and green space Booked ahead
  7. Our quick route: the Administration Pavilion's pink vaulted ceilings and stained-glass staircase, the Central Courtyard and the coloured dome of the Sant Salvador Pavilion, a photo of the circular glass Operating Theatre, then out through the white-tiled underground tunnels
  8. 2:15 PMUber to Park Güell
  9. 2:30–4:30 PMPark Güell
  10. 4:30 PMOptional drink and a bite: Restaurant Terra Mía, Comida de Olla or Bodega La Riera
  11. 5:15 PMTaxi to Bunkers del Carmel (Turó de la Rovira), only if the sky is clear. It's just a high point, but the panorama is spectacular.
  12. Back to the hotel to freshen up, with a drink at the rooftop bar
  13. 8:00 PMDrinks nearby first, then Uber or taxi to dinner
  14. 9:00 PMDinner at Casa Amàlia 1950 Booked ahead

Tip: Sagrada Família dress code: shoulders and knees covered, hats and caps off.

Day 7 Wed, Jul 8 · Mallorca

Our one tour of the week

Our one booked day out was a tour of the Serra de Tramuntana on Mallorca's west coast: the cobbled, flower-lined lanes of Valldemossa, the Sa Foradada lookout at Son Marroig, the hillside village of Deià, lunch by the harbour in Port de Sóller and a wander round Sóller's main square, then one last view over the Bay of Palma on the way back.

Our tips

  • Dress for Sagrada Família

    Shoulders and knees need to be covered, and hats and caps come off inside.

  • Go early for the light

    The 9–10 AM Quiet Hour is calm, and around 10:00 the sun streams through the Nativity facade's windows. Spend the first hour on the east side of the nave before the sun climbs.

  • Head to the tower early, with a coin

    Lines for the Passion Tower elevator back up, so start walking about ten minutes before your slot. Bags go in free lockers at the base, which need a 1-euro coin.

  • Check which cable car you want

    There's more than one cable car option in Barcelona, so check which one goes where you want. The Port Cable Car runs from the waterfront up to Montjuïc; if the line is long, take a taxi instead.

  • Getting to Port Vell

    Give your driver Moll de la Fusta, the wharf, as the address.

  • Watch the weather for Bunkers del Carmel

    It's only worth the trip on a clear day; otherwise the hotel rooftop pool is a fine alternative.

  • Eating near Park Güell

    Avoid anywhere with "Güell" in the name.

  • Check opening hours

    If you want to add Montjuïc Castle at the end of a day, check its closing time first.

  • Confirm tour pickups

    Confirm the pickup details for island tours ahead of time.
