By Tony & Sharon · Traveled April 2025

Two capitals, one spring break. We spent four nights at The Bailey's Hotel in South Kensington, close enough to walk to Kensington Palace, Hyde Park and the big museums, and used the days for the classics: the Churchill War Rooms, Westminster, Borough Market, the Tower of London, Tower Bridge and a night at Hamilton. One whole afternoon and evening went to the Warner Bros. Studio Tour, with dinner in the Great Hall.

Then the Eurostar took us from St Pancras to Paris in a little over two and a half hours. We based ourselves at Hotel Atmospheres in the Latin Quarter, walked to Sainte-Chapelle and Notre-Dame the afternoon we arrived, and used the Paris Museum Pass for the Louvre, the Orangerie, the Musée d'Orsay, Les Invalides and the Panthéon. The last full day was Versailles in the morning and the Latin Quarter in the afternoon.

We pre-booked everything with a fixed time (meals, timed museum entries, the Eiffel Tower, the cruise) and left the gaps loose, with a short list of options for lunch and shopping so we could decide on the day.

The plan, day by day

Day 1 Sat, Apr 12 · London

Hyde Park, Kensington Palace & the museums

We land early and keep the first day entirely on foot around our hotel: the gardens of Kensington and Hyde Park, afternoon tea and a tour at Kensington Palace, then one of the big South Kensington museums before dinner around the corner.

  1. 8:00 AMLand in London and take a hired car to The Bailey's Hotel
  2. 9:30 AMCheck in at The Bailey's Hotel and leave our luggage, since the room won't be ready yet. Freshen up and head out Booked ahead
  3. 10:00 AMCoffee and pastries at the hotel, or whatever we find on the walk
  4. 10:20 AMWalk through Kensington Gardens: the Italian Gardens and their fountains, the Albert Memorial across from the Royal Albert Hall, and the spring flower displays in the Kensington Palace Gardens
  5. If there's time, carry on into Hyde Park for the swans on the Serpentine, the two free Serpentine Galleries, the Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Fountain and the Rose Garden in the southeast corner
  6. 12:00 PMAfternoon tea for lunch at The Orangery at Kensington Palace: scones, sandwiches and pastries in a royal setting Booked ahead
  7. 1:30 PMTour Kensington Palace (about 1.5 hours): Queen Victoria's childhood rooms, the King's and Queen's State Apartments, and the Princess Diana fashion exhibition if it's on. Our Historic Royal Palaces membership card gets us in, with no timed ticket needed
  8. 3:00 PMWalk 15 minutes to South Kensington's museums
  9. 3:15 PMNatural History Museum for the dinosaurs, gemstones and that architecture (free timed tickets, closes at 5:50 PM). The plan B is the Victoria & Albert Museum for fashion and design (free, no ticket needed, closes at 5:45 PM) Booked ahead
  10. 5:30 PMPortuguese custard tarts and espresso at Café de Nata, 25 Cromwell Place
  11. 5:45 PMBack to the hotel to rest
  12. 7:00 PMDinner at Rocca, 73 Old Brompton Road, for classic Italian comfort food, about a 10-minute walk. Honest Burger is the fallback if we're too tired for a sit-down meal Booked ahead
  13. 9:00 PMWalk back to The Bailey's Hotel

Tip: Even with an early arrival, a day of walking-distance sights is the best cure for jet lag. Nothing today needs a train.

Day 2 Sun, Apr 13 · London

Churchill War Rooms, Westminster, Borough Market & the South Bank

Our biggest walking day in London. We start underground in Churchill's wartime bunker, walk through St. James's Park to Buckingham Palace and Westminster, eat our way around Borough Market, then follow the river past Tate Modern and the Globe and cross the Millennium Bridge to St. Paul's.

  1. 8:15 AMBreakfast at the hotel
  2. 9:00 AMTake the Tube to the Churchill War Rooms, King Charles Street
  3. 9:30 AMTour the Churchill War Rooms: Winston Churchill's wartime bunker, the Cabinet War Rooms and the Churchill Museum (timed entry between 9:30 and 10:00 AM) Booked ahead
  4. 11:30 AMWalk through St. James's Park to Buckingham Palace, about 15 minutes, and spend 20 minutes on photos at the Victoria Memorial and the palace gates
  5. 12:00 PMWalk 12 minutes to Westminster Abbey for photos outside (we didn't have tickets to go in)
  6. 12:30 PMThe Houses of Parliament and Big Ben from Parliament Square, three minutes away, then out onto Westminster Bridge for the views along the Thames
  7. 1:15 PMUber to Borough Market
  8. 1:30 PMLunch by grazing around Borough Market: chorizo and rocket rolls from Brindisa, the legendary grilled cheese at Kappacasein, doughnuts from Bread Ahead and the chocolate-covered strawberry stand
  9. 4:00 PMWalk 10 minutes along the river to Tate Modern
  10. 4:15 PMExplore Tate Modern, one of the world's best modern art museums, with Picasso, Warhol, Hockney and more. Entry is free (some special exhibitions are ticketed) and it closes at 6:00 PM
  11. 5:30 PMPhotos outside Shakespeare's Globe
  12. 5:45 PMWalk across the Millennium Bridge, about 10 minutes, stopping for the classic photo of the Thames and St. Paul's dome
  13. 6:00 PMSt. Paul's Cathedral from outside. There are no visitor tickets on Sundays and a 5:30 PM service is on, so this is a photo stop we can skip if we're tired
  14. 6:15 PMHead back to the hotel to rest
  15. 9:00 PMDinner at Flat Iron, 17–18 Henrietta Street in Covent Garden, a steak place with great reviews. Our backup was a 7:15 PM table at the Cadogan Arms on King's Road, a 15-minute walk from the hotel Booked ahead
  16. 10:30 PMTube back to The Bailey's Hotel

Tip: We held a second, closer dinner reservation in case we were worn out. The Cadogan Arms books through SevenRooms and charges if you don't cancel 24 hours ahead, so decide the day before.

Day 3 Mon, Apr 14 · London

The Tower of London, Tower Bridge & Hamilton

History in the morning and theatre at night. We're at the Tower of London when it opens, see the Crown Jewels before the crowds, join a Yeoman Warder tour, then walk out onto Tower Bridge. After an early theatre-menu dinner near the hotel, it's Hamilton in the West End.

  1. 8:00 AMBreakfast at the hotel
  2. 9:00 AMHalf an hour by Tube or car to the Tower of London. Our Historic Royal Palaces membership card gets us in, with no timed ticket needed
  3. 10:00 AMTour the Tower when it opens, in this order: the Crown Jewels, a Yeoman Warder tour (the first is at 10:30, so we join the next one after the jewels), the White Tower, the Bloody Tower, Tower Green and the execution site, then the ravens and a last walk around the walls. Allow 2–3 hours
  4. 1:00 PMLunch at Byward Kitchen & Bar on Byward Street, three minutes from the Tower (booked on their website) Booked ahead
  5. 2:00 PMWalk 10 minutes to Tower Bridge
  6. 2:10 PMTower Bridge Exhibition: the glass walkway with the Thames beneath your feet, the Victorian engine rooms that lift the bridge, and panoramic views for photos (2:10 PM tickets, entry until 2:30) Booked ahead
  7. 3:30 PMBack to the hotel to rest before the theatre
  8. 5:15 PMDinner at Steak & Company, 152 Gloucester Road, two minutes from the hotel, on the theatre set menu with happy hour. We need to be done by 6:45 Booked ahead
  9. 6:45 PMUber to the Victoria Palace Theatre
  10. 7:30 PMHamilton at the Victoria Palace Theatre Booked ahead
  11. 10:30 PMUber back to The Bailey's Hotel

Tip: Go to the Crown Jewels first thing. The line grows fast once the tour groups arrive, and the Yeoman Warder tours run regularly, so you can always join the next one.

Day 4 Tue, Apr 15 · London

Notting Hill & the Harry Potter Studio Tour

A slow morning among the pastel houses of Notting Hill, a rest at the hotel, then the afternoon and evening at the Warner Bros. Studio Tour in Leavesden, with dinner at the Hogwarts tables in the Great Hall.

  1. 8:00 AMBreakfast at the hotel
  2. 9:00 AMTravel to Notting Hill
  3. 9:30 AMExplore Notting Hill: the antique shops, boutiques and colorful storefronts of Portobello Road Market, the pastel houses of St. Luke's Mews (including the pink house from Love Actually), the Notting Hill Bookshop from the movie, and Hillgate Place, one of the best streets for photos of the painted houses
  4. 11:30 AMLunch in Notting Hill. Our shortlist: Gold for European-Mediterranean pastas, seafood and wood-fired pizza; Farmacy for organic, plant-based bowls and burgers; or Eggbreak for a relaxed all-day brunch
  5. 12:45 PMWalk 5 minutes to Notting Hill Gate and take the Circle line eastbound to Gloucester Road (a 5-minute ride), then walk back to the hotel by 1:00 to rest and freshen up
  6. 1:30 PMTravel to the Warner Bros. Studio Tour in Leavesden, arriving to check in around 3:00
  7. 3:30 PMThe Making of Harry Potter studio tour begins. Highlights: the Great Hall, the Forbidden Forest, Platform 9¾ and the Hogwarts Express, Diagon Alley with Ollivanders and Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes, Gringotts and its fire-breathing dragon, the Ministry of Magic, the 1:24 scale Hogwarts Castle model, the creature effects workshop and the green-screen broomstick ride Booked ahead
  8. 6:30 PMDinner at the Hogwarts tables in the Great Hall Booked ahead
  9. 8:00 PMBack to exploring the studio tour
  10. 9:00 PMLeave for London, back at The Bailey's Hotel around 10:30

Tip: Book the studio tour with the Hogwarts dinner if it's offered for your date. Eating in the Great Hall is the highlight, and it splits a long visit nicely in two.

Day 5 Wed, Apr 16 · London → Paris

Breakfast in the Sky Garden, the Eurostar & a first evening in Paris

A last breakfast with a view over London, then the Eurostar to Paris. We drop our bags in the Latin Quarter and walk straight out to Sainte-Chapelle, Notre-Dame and Île Saint-Louis, ending with a cheese-heavy dinner on Rue Mouffetard.

  1. 7:30 AMUber to the Sky Garden, arriving early to go through security and take in the gardens and the views over the City
  2. 8:30 AMBreakfast at Darwin Brasserie in the Sky Garden Booked ahead
  3. 10:15 AMUber back to the hotel
  4. 10:45 AMCheck out of The Bailey's Hotel
  5. 11:00 AMHired car from Cheam Limousines to St Pancras International Booked ahead
  6. 12:31 PMEurostar from London St Pancras to Paris Gare du Nord in Comfort First Class (printed tickets, Car 1, Seats 63 and 64) Booked ahead
  7. 4:00 PMArrive at Gare du Nord and take a car to the hotel
  8. 4:30 PMCheck in at Hotel Atmospheres in the Latin Quarter Booked ahead
  9. 4:45 PMWalk 15 minutes to Sainte-Chapelle
  10. 5:00 PMSainte-Chapelle and its breathtaking stained glass (30–45 minutes; last entry 5:30 PM)
  11. 5:45 PMWalk 5 minutes to Notre-Dame Cathedral, in time for the 6:00 PM Mass. Entry is free with a reserved slot: book online a day or two ahead, or try the same day and check the live wait time in the app. The archaeological crypt below is an optional extra
  12. 6:30 PMCross the Pont Saint-Louis to Île Saint-Louis, stroll Rue Saint-Louis en l'Île and get Berthillon ice cream (most of its counters close around 8:00 PM)
  13. 7:30 PMWalk along the Seine toward the Pont Neuf
  14. 7:45 PMInto the Latin Quarter: a photo outside Shakespeare and Company (closes at 8:00 PM). The Abbey Bookshop nearby is open 10–7 (Sundays 2–7), so we saved it for another day. Freshen up at the hotel if there's time
  15. 9:00 PMDinner at Le Vieux Bistrot, 54 Rue Mouffetard, where it's all about the cheese. A 10-minute walk from the hotel (booked on their website) Booked ahead
  16. 10:30 PMEvening walk back to the hotel

Tip: Eurostar check-in closes 30 minutes before departure, and there's passport control for both countries at St Pancras, so give yourself more time than that.

Day 6 Thu, Apr 17 · Paris

The Louvre, Rue Saint-Honoré & Monet's Water Lilies

We're at the Louvre before it opens for the big hitters, then spend the afternoon on the elegant streets around it: lunch, shopping, the Water Lilies at the Orangerie and a sit in the Tuileries before a classic French dinner in Saint-Germain.

  1. 9:00 AMArrive at the Louvre to be among the first inside
  2. 9:30 AMThe Louvre Museum with the Museum Pass and a reserved 9:30 entry. Our priorities: the Mona Lisa, the Venus de Milo, the Winged Victory of Samothrace and the Napoleon III Apartments Booked ahead
  3. 12:30 PMStroll through the Palais Royal Gardens
  4. 1:00 PMLunch near Rue Saint-Honoré. Our shortlist: Café Kitsuné at the Palais Royal or Le Fumoir across from the Louvre for something casual, Le Meurice or Bistrot Richelieu for classic French, or crêpes from a street stand
  5. 2:30 PMShopping and exploring. Options: the luxury houses of Rue Saint-Honoré, Fragonard and Diptyque for perfume, The Frankie Shop and Maison Kitsuné, the jewelers of Place Vendôme, the covered Passage Vivienne (10 minutes from the Louvre), or Saint-Germain-des-Prés for Maje, Sandro and Comptoir des Cotonniers
  6. 4:30 PMMusée de l'Orangerie for Monet's Water Lilies and its impressionist collection (Museum Pass, no timed ticket needed)
  7. 5:30 PMRelax in the Jardin des Tuileries
  8. 7:30 PMDinner at Joséphine Chez Dumonet, 117 Rue du Cherche-Midi. The no-reservations alternative is Le Relais de l'Entrecôte on Rue Saint-Benoît for steak frites, but expect a queue Booked ahead

Tip: Even with a timed ticket and a Museum Pass, arrive at the Louvre 30 minutes early. Head straight for the Mona Lisa, then work outward while the rest of the museum is still quiet.

Day 7 Fri, Apr 18 · Paris

Musée d'Orsay, Les Invalides, the Eiffel Tower & a Seine dinner cruise

Impressionists first thing, Napoleon's tomb before lunch on Rue Cler, the Eiffel Tower summit in the afternoon, and a dinner cruise on the Seine to finish, with the tower sparkling on the way home.

  1. 8:30 AMArrive at the Musée d'Orsay 30 minutes before it opens (Museum Pass, no timed ticket needed)
  2. 9:00 AMExplore the museum from the top floor down: Monet, Van Gogh, Degas and Renoir, and Van Gogh's Starry Night Over the Rhône (1888). There's a quick ground-floor café if we need a break
  3. 11:00 AMWalk across the Pont Alexandre III, one of the most beautiful bridges in Paris
  4. 11:30 AMLes Invalides and Napoleon's Tomb (Museum Pass)
  5. 1:00 PMLunch at Le Petit Cler, 29 Rue Cler, for charming, casual French classics. Afterward, pop into the cheese, chocolate and wine shops along Rue Cler Booked ahead
  6. 2:30 PMWalk to the Eiffel Tower and go through security
  7. 3:30 PMEiffel Tower summit visit (3:30 PM tickets), about two hours at the top and on the way down Booked ahead
  8. 5:30 PMA snack on the way to the cruise if we're hungry: pastries and apple tart at Boulangerie Poilâne, or hot chocolate at Carette in Trocadéro
  9. 7:15 PMArrive at Bateaux Mouches, Port de la Conférence
  10. 7:30 PMDinner cruise on the Seine with live music. We board at 7:30, the boat leaves at 8:30 and we're back around 10:45 (booked on Tripadvisor) Booked ahead
  11. 11:00 PMWalk to see the Eiffel Tower sparkle, then back to the hotel

Tip: The Eiffel Tower sparkles for five minutes at the start of every hour after dark, so you don't need to plan much to catch it.

Day 8 Sat, Apr 19 · Versailles & Paris

Versailles & the Latin Quarter

An early train to Versailles for the palace, the Hall of Mirrors, the gardens and the Trianon, then back to our own neighborhood for the Panthéon, the Luxembourg Gardens and Rue Mouffetard, and a last dinner in Paris.

  1. 7:30 AMBreakfast at Hotel Atmospheres
  2. 8:00 AMRER C train from Saint-Michel Notre-Dame to Versailles Château Rive Gauche, about an hour
  3. 9:00 AMTour the Palace of Versailles and the Hall of Mirrors with the audio guide (9:00 AM tickets), then the gardens and the musical garden show (it has its own entrance if you're re-entering) Booked ahead
  4. 12:00 PMThe Estate of Trianon (tickets from noon) Booked ahead
  5. 1:15 PMRER C train back to Paris, about an hour
  6. 2:30 PMLunch near the Panthéon. Our shortlist: Le Coupe-Chou, a cozy historic French restaurant; La Maison de Verlaine, a charming bistro; or L'Avant Comptoir for lively, tapas-style plates
  7. 3:30 PMThe Panthéon (Museum Pass, no timed ticket; closes at 6:30 PM): the tombs of Voltaire, Rousseau and Marie Curie, and Foucault's Pendulum
  8. 4:30 PMWalk 5 minutes to the Jardin du Luxembourg for a peaceful stroll past its fountains, the Medici Fountain and the gardens
  9. 5:30 PMRue Mouffetard market street: food shops, bakeries and specialty stores, plus a snack of cream puffs from Odette or a sweet or savory tart from La Maison des Tartes
  10. 6:30 PMFree time for bookstores (the Abbey Bookshop is open until 7) or another neighborhood
  11. 8:30 PMDinner at La Maison de Verlaine, 39 Rue Descartes (booked on their website) Booked ahead
  12. 10:30 PMEvening walk back to the hotel

Tip: Get to the Versailles station 15–20 minutes before you need to leave, and buy RER tickets at the machines (some take contactless cards).

Our tips

  • Consider a Historic Royal Palaces membership

    Kensington Palace and the Tower of London are both Historic Royal Palaces. Our membership card got us into both with no timed ticket, so we could walk in when it suited the day.

  • Let the Paris Museum Pass do the work

    It covered the Louvre, the Orangerie, the Musée d'Orsay, Les Invalides and the Panthéon. Some places, like the Louvre, still need a free timed reservation, so check each one before you go.

  • Use contactless on the Tube

    A contactless card or phone works on every London Underground line, with no Oyster card needed. Avoid the rush hours of about 8–9:30 AM and 5–6:30 PM on weekdays, and remember the last trains run around midnight (earlier on Sundays).

  • Print the Eurostar tickets

    We kept printed tickets handy for boarding. Check-in closes 30 minutes before departure, and both UK and French passport checks happen at St Pancras before you board.

  • Download Citymapper

    It plans journeys in both cities. TfL Go is the official London app, and RATP and Bonjour RATP cover Paris. Uber works in both, which is handy late at night or with luggage.

  • Hold a backup dinner

    After a long day of walking, a reservation across town can feel like a lot. Where we could, we kept a closer option in our pocket. Just note each one's cancellation window.

  • Late nights in Paris

    The Paris Metro runs until about 1 AM, and until about 2 AM on Fridays and Saturdays. Dinner reservations at 8:30 or 9 PM are completely normal.

  • Budget-friendly shopping

    Beyond Rue Saint-Honoré, try the Marais for boutiques and vintage, Galeries Lafayette and Printemps for every price point, and Rue Montorgueil for local shops and pastries. In London, South Kensington, Covent Garden and Notting Hill were our favorites.
